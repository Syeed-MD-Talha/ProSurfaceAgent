# Timber and Wood Repair

Repair, restoration, and refinishing for non-floor timber: doors, furniture, cabinets, and window frames in Singapore homes and businesses.

Source pages: https://www.prosurfaceworks.com/services/timber-wood-repair/, https://www.prosurfaceworks.com/faq/

## When to use

Use when the user asks about wooden doors, furniture, cabinets, window frames, peeling varnish, scratches, water marks, timber restoration. If it is a parquet FLOOR, use parquet-flooring instead.

## Work covered

Not three separate services to pick upfront. Matched to the actual damage:

- Repair: physical damage or a specific defective area, without touching the rest.
- Restoration: broader work improving appearance and condition of older or worn timber.
- Refinishing: new finish after the old one deteriorated and the surface was prepared.

## Surfaces

- Wooden doors: scratches, worn finish, warping from use and humidity.
- Furniture: tables, cabinetry, faded, scratched, lost finish.
- Cabinets: kitchen and built-in, worn, chipped, discoloured.
- Window frames: timber frames exposed to weather and moisture, peel and deteriorate faster.
- Other timber considered case by case.

## Decision guide

- Surface-level damage on sound timber -> usually repairable.
- Localised scratch or chip -> repair is usually enough.
- Whole finish deteriorated -> refinishing more likely.
- Severely compromised or structural damage -> replacement may be more practical, confirmed on-site.
- Peeling varnish must be stripped before a new coat. Revarnishing over failed coat will not hold.

## Process

Assess timber type, extent of damage, existing finish, and whether repair or replacement is practical. Ronseal and Nippon finishes are used where revarnishing is required.

## Worked example

User: "My wooden door varnish is peeling."
Answer: "That is a failed finish, so it needs stripping before a new coat. The team would assess the door, remove the old varnish, then revarnish. Share photos for a free assessment."
