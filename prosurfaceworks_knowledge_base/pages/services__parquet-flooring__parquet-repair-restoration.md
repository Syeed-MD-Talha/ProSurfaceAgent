What Is Parquet Repair & Restoration?
Assessing the damage present in an existing parquet floor
Repairing damaged areas

Common Parquet Flooring Problems We Assess
Damaged parquet blocks — individual pieces that are cracked, chipped or broken
Loose parquet pieces — sections that have come away from the subfloor
Missing sections — gaps where parquet pieces are absent
Cracks or splits — in individual pieces or across sections
Surface deterioration — general breakdown of the wood or finish
Localised damage — problems confined to specific areas of the floor
Uneven or deteriorated areas — sections that stand out from the rest of the floor
Not every type of parquet damage can be repaired in the same way, which is why each floor is assessed individually.

Can All Parquet Damage Be Repaired?
No, not all parquet damage is repairable in the same way. The result depends on:
The severity of the damage
The extent of the deterioration
The condition of the surrounding parquet
The condition of the subfloor
Whether matching replacement material is available
Any moisture or underlying issues present

On an older floor, a replacement piece may not be perfectly identical to the surrounding parquet, particularly where the existing floor has aged or faded unevenly. This is discussed as part of the assessment.
Parquet Damage Caused by Moisture
Where parquet damage is related to moisture, identifying the source is an important part of the assessment. Replacing the damaged wood alone may not resolve the underlying issue, and damage can recur if the source is not addressed.
Pro Surface Works does not provide waterproofing or plumbing repair services. Where a moisture source needs to be addressed, this would need to be handled separately.
Repair or Replace the Parquet Floor?
Repair may be considered when:
The damage is localised
The majority of the floor remains usable
Matching replacement material is available
More extensive work may be needed when:
Damage is widespread
Underlying problems exist
Large sections are severely deteriorated

FAQ
Frequently Asked Questions
Can damaged parquet flooring be repaired?
In many cases, yes, but this depends on the severity and type of damage, along with the condition of the surrounding floor. An assessment confirms what is possible.
When should parquet be repaired instead of polished?
When there is physical damage such as loose, missing or cracked pieces, rather than just dullness or loss of shine. See
Parquet Floor Polishing
for lighter surface wear.
Can individual parquet pieces be replaced?
Yes, individual pieces can often be replaced, though matching the existing pattern, wood type and appearance depends on the specific floor.
Can water-damaged parquet be restored?
This depends on the extent of the damage and whether the moisture source has been identified and addressed. Replacing damaged wood alone may not resolve an ongoing moisture issue.
Is it better to repair parquet or replace the entire floor?
This depends on how widespread the damage is and whether matching replacement material is available. There is no single answer that applies to every floor.
How long does parquet floor repair take?
This depends on the extent of the damage and the area involved, and is confirmed after the assessment.
