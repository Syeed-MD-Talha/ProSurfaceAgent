What Is Vinyl Flooring?
Vinyl flooring is a modern flooring material available in wood- and stone-inspired finishes, used across both residential and commercial interiors.
It's a common choice where a durable, low-maintenance surface is needed without the cost or upkeep of natural materials.
Vinyl Flooring Options for Different Spaces
SPC (Rigid-Core Vinyl)
A rigid-core vinyl flooring option suited to areas where a firmer, more stable underfoot feel is preferred.
LVT (Luxury Vinyl Tile)
A vinyl flooring option available in a range of wood- and stone-inspired finishes, suited to a variety of interior styles.
Vinyl Planks
Plank-format vinyl flooring, commonly used to replicate the look of timber flooring.
Which option suits your space depends on the room, the expected use and the finish you're looking for, and this is discussed as part of the assessment.

Our Vinyl Flooring Installation Process
1. Site Assessment
The existing floor, room condition and installation requirements are assessed.
2. Measurement & Planning
The area is measured and the layout is planned.
3. Subfloor Assessment
The existing floor's level, soundness, surface condition and, where relevant, any moisture-related concerns are checked.
4. Surface Preparation
Where required, the surface is cleaned, patched, levelled, or unsuitable existing flooring is removed.
5. Vinyl Installation
The flooring is installed using the appropriate installation system for the selected product.
6. Edge & Transition Finishing
Where applicable, skirting, edge trims, thresholds and transitions are finished.
7. Final Inspection
Alignment, joints and seams, edges, surface appearance and overall finish are checked.
Why Proper Subfloor Preparation Matters
Vinyl flooring is relatively thin, so irregularities in the surface beneath it can affect the final appearance and feel of the finished floor.
Uneven areas, surface defects, existing damage and moisture concerns are all things worth assessing before installation, though the extent of preparation needed depends on the specific floor.
Can Vinyl Flooring Be Installed Over an Existing Floor?
In some cases, vinyl can be installed over an existing floor, but suitability depends on the condition, level and stability of the existing surface.

FAQ
Frequently Asked Questions
What is vinyl flooring installation?
It is the process of fitting vinyl flooring, such as SPC, LVT or vinyl planks, into a room or space, from site assessment and subfloor preparation through to installation and finishing.
Can vinyl flooring be installed over existing tiles?
In some cases, yes, though this depends on the condition, level and stability of the existing tiles. This is confirmed during the site assessment.
Does the floor need to be level before vinyl installation?
A level, sound surface generally gives a better result, since vinyl is relatively thin and can show irregularities beneath it. The amount of preparation needed depends on the existing floor's condition.
How long does vinyl flooring installation take?
This depends on the floor area, the installation method and how much preparation the existing floor requires, and is confirmed after assessment.
Is vinyl flooring suitable for HDB flats?
Yes, vinyl flooring is commonly installed in HDB flats as well as condominiums and landed homes, subject to the specific room and existing floor condition.
Is vinyl flooring suitable for commercial spaces?
Yes, vinyl flooring is used in offices, retail spaces, showrooms and other commercial environments, with the product specification chosen to suit expected foot traffic and use.
Can vinyl flooring be installed in high-traffic areas?
Yes, though the appropriate product and installation approach depend on the level of traffic and how the space is used.
How should vinyl flooring be maintained?
Regular cleaning, prompt removal of dirt and spills, and following the manufacturer's recommendations for the specific product will help keep a vinyl floor in good condition.
What happens if the existing floor is uneven?
Uneven areas are identified during the subfloor assessment, and appropriate preparation, such as patching or levelling, is carried out where needed before installation.
