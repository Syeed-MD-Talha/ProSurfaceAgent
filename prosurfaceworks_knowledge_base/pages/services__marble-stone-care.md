RECOGNISE THE PROBLEM
Common Problems With Marble & Stone Surfaces
“My marble floor has lost its shine.” This is usually surface wear or accumulated residue rather than damage, and it's often solved with polishing.
“There are scratches on the countertop.” Light scratches are typically cosmetic and treatable; deeper ones may need restoration rather than polishing.
“There's a dull patch where something acidic spilled.” This is etching, a reaction with the stone itself, and it needs restoration rather than a surface clean.
“The stone looks stained or discoloured.” Staining depends on what caused it and how long it's been there, confirmed during an on-site assessment.
“Some areas look more worn than others.” High-traffic spots wear faster than the rest of the floor, which is common near entrances and hallways.
DECISION GUIDE
Can Marble Be Restored Instead of Replaced?
In many cases, yes. Worn or dull marble can usually be restored, but the right option depends on the depth and type of damage.
Restoration may be considered when: the wear is mainly cosmetic, scratches or etching are within a treatable depth, and the stone is still structurally sound.
Replacement may be considered when: the damage is extensive, the stone is structurally compromised, or restoration is unlikely to give a practical result.

FAQ
Frequently Asked Questions
Can all marble floors be restored?
Most marble floors can be restored to some degree, but the outcome depends on how deep the damage is and the condition of the stone underneath. A site assessment confirms whether polishing, restoration, or a more targeted treatment is the right fit before any work begins.
How do I know whether my marble needs polishing or restoration?
If the floor has simply dulled with everyday wear, polishing is usually enough. If there are scratches, etching, cracks, or deeper stains, restoration is typically needed first. The team can confirm which applies during a free assessment.
Can scratches and etching be removed from marble?
Light scratches and etching can often be treated through restoration, which addresses the surface rather than just adding shine. Deeper damage may limit how much can be corrected, which is why the actual depth of the mark matters more than how it looks at first glance.
How often should marble floors be maintained?
This depends on foot traffic, location, and how the floor is cleaned day to day. Commercial spaces with heavy traffic typically need attention more often than a quiet residential room, so there's no fixed schedule that applies to every property.
Can kitchen countertops and vanity tops be polished?
Yes. Kitchen countertops and bathroom vanity or basin tops are polished using the same principles as floors, adjusted for the material and how the surface is used day to day.
Does Pro Surface Works work with other stone surfaces?
Marble is the main focus of this category, along with granite and solid-surface materials on countertops and vanity tops. Other hard floor types, such as granite or terrazzo flooring, homogeneous tile, and concrete, are covered separately under General Floor Care.
