What Is Old Varnish Removal?
Old varnish removal addresses the existing coating on a parquet floor, which can deteriorate over time through wear, age or exposure to moisture and cleaning products.
Assessing the existing varnish or coating
Addressing or removing that coating using an appropriate method
Preparing the underlying parquet for its next treatment
Not every floor calls for the same removal method, since this depends on the existing coating and the condition of the parquet beneath it.
Varnish removal is not the same as complete floor restoration. It addresses the existing coating specifically, and what happens after removal depends on the condition found underneath.
When Should Old Varnish Be Removed from Parquet Flooring?
The existing varnish is heavily worn
The coating has deteriorated
The finish has become uneven
The previous coating is no longer suitable for the intended treatment
The surface has accumulated multiple layers of old finish
The existing coating needs to be addressed before a new finishing system is applied
Not all old varnish needs to be removed. Whether removal is appropriate depends on the specific condition of the floor, which is why each case is assessed individually.
Signs Your Parquet's Existing Varnish May Need Attention
Peeling or flaking finish
Uneven sheen across the floor
Worn traffic areas
Visible coating deterioration
Discolouration
A surface that no longer responds well to normal maintenance
Why Proper Varnish Removal Matters
It allows the underlying parquet to be properly assessed
It prepares the surface appropriately for whatever treatment follows
It avoids working over incompatible or deteriorated coating layers
It supports a more consistent result where refinishing follows
Proper preparation can help create a more suitable surface for the next treatment, depending on the condition of the parquet. It does not guarantee a particular final finish, which depends on the floor's underlying condition.
Assessing the Existing Varnish Before Removal
The type and condition of the existing coating, where identifiable
The number and condition of coating layers, where relevant
Surface wear
The condition of the underlying wood or parquet
Any previous treatments applied to the floor
Areas with different levels of deterioration
This assessment is what determines the appropriate removal method for a specific floor, rather than applying the same approach to every job.

Does Removing Old Varnish Always Require Sanding?
Not necessarily. Whether sanding is needed depends on:
The existing coating
The floor's overall condition
The desired final treatment
The thickness of the coating
The previous finish
The condition of the parquet itself
There is no single method that applies to every floor. The appropriate approach is confirmed during assessment.

FAQ
Frequently Asked Questions
What is old varnish removal?
It is the process of addressing and removing a parquet floor's existing coating, preparing the underlying surface for an appropriate next treatment.
Why does old varnish need to be removed?
Old varnish is removed when it has deteriorated, become uneven, or is no longer suitable for the treatment a floor needs next.
Can old varnish be removed from parquet flooring?
In most cases, yes, though the appropriate method depends on the type and condition of the existing coating.
Does removing old varnish damage parquet?
The appropriate removal approach depends on the existing coating and the parquet's condition beneath it. Professional assessment is important before choosing a method.
Does varnish removal always require sanding?
Not always. Whether sanding is needed depends on the existing coating, the floor's condition, and the treatment planned to follow.
What happens after old varnish is removed?
The floor is reassessed and a suitable next treatment is recommended, such as polishing, sanding and varnishing, or repair and restoration if damage is found.
Should I polish my parquet or remove the existing varnish?
This depends on whether the existing finish is still basically sound. If it is, polishing may be enough. If the coating itself has deteriorated, removal is usually the more appropriate starting point.
How long does varnish removal take?
This depends on the size of the area and the condition of the existing coating, and is confirmed after assessment.
