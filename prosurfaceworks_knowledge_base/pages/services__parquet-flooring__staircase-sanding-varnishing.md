What Is Staircase Sanding & Varnishing?
Assessing the existing staircase surface and finish
Preparing worn or old surfaces
Sanding the treads, risers and other wood surfaces
Applying a suitable varnish or finish
Renewing the staircase's appearance and finish
The specific approach depends on the staircase's construction and existing condition, confirmed during assessment.
When Should You Consider Staircase Sanding & Varnishing?
Stair treads look worn
The finish has become dull
The existing varnish has deteriorated
The surface appears uneven
High-use areas of the staircase show visible wear
The staircase no longer matches the desired interior finish
Where a staircase has structural damage rather than just a worn finish, this would need to be considered separately as a repair matter.
Why Staircase Sanding Requires Careful Work
Individual stair treads, each needing consistent treatment
Risers, which are handled separately from the treads
Edges and corners, which need careful attention
Handrails and other wood components, where applicable
Difficult-to-access areas that a flat floor does not present
Achieving a consistent finish across every step
Working safely around a staircase that remains part of an active property
A staircase's geometry and step-by-step structure call for a different working approach than a large, flat floor area.
Staircase Sanding vs Floor Sanding
Floor Sanding
Covers larger, horizontal floor surfaces.
Staircase Sanding
Covers individual steps, edges, corners and vertical surfaces such as risers, where applicable.

Our Staircase Sanding & Varnishing Process
1. Staircase Assessment

5. Varnishing

6. Drying / Curing
The applied finish is left to dry and cure according to the product used.
7. Final Inspection
The finish and consistency across all steps are inspected.

FAQ
Frequently Asked Questions
What is staircase sanding and varnishing?
It is a process that sands and refinishes a worn wooden or parquet staircase, treating the treads, risers, edges and other surfaces to renew the finish.
Can old varnish be removed from a wooden staircase?
Sanding removes or refines existing varnish as part of preparing the staircase for a new finish. Where old varnish removal is the specific concern, see
Old Varnish Removal
How long does staircase sanding and varnishing take?
This depends on the size of the staircase, its existing condition, and the finish selected, and is confirmed after assessment.
Can every wooden staircase be sanded and varnished?
This depends on the staircase's material and existing condition. Where there is structural damage rather than surface wear, this needs to be addressed first. See
Parquet Repair & Restoration
Is staircase sanding different from floor sanding?
Yes. Floor sanding covers larger, horizontal surfaces, while staircase sanding covers individual steps, edges, corners and vertical surfaces such as risers. See
Parquet Floor Sanding & Varnishing
for floor-wide treatment.
How should a newly varnished staircase be maintained?
Follow the recommended curing period, avoid excessive moisture, use suitable cleaning products and protect high-contact areas such as tread edges.
