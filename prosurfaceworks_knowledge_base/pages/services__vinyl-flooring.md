MATERIAL GUIDE
SPC or LVT: Choosing Vinyl Flooring for Your Space
SPC (stone plastic composite) is the more rigid, water-resistant option, generally suited to areas with higher moisture exposure or heavier daily use. LVT (luxury vinyl tile) is more flexible and often chosen for its appearance and feel underfoot, and tends to suit lower-traffic residential rooms.
Which one fits depends on the area of use, expected foot traffic, the condition of the existing subfloor, and how the space needs to look and feel. RECOGNISE THE PROBLEM
What Can Go Wrong With Vinyl Flooring?
“One section of my vinyl floor is lifting.” This is often related to moisture underneath or a weakened adhesive bond, and it usually gets worse if left alone.
“There are scratches and scuff marks near the entrance.” High-traffic areas near doors wear faster than the rest of the room, which is normal but still fixable.
“A section has torn or split.” Sharp impacts or heavy furniture can tear vinyl, and whether that section can be patched depends on where it is and how large the tear is.
“The colour looks patchy or faded in places.” Sunlight exposure and uneven wear can cause this, and it's more noticeable on some vinyl types than others.
DECISION GUIDE
Should Vinyl Flooring Be Repaired or Replaced?
It depends on the extent and location of the damage, the condition of the surrounding flooring, and whether a practical repair is possible.
Repair may be considered when: the damage is limited, the affected section can be addressed on its own, and the surrounding flooring is still in usable condition.
Replacement may be considered when: the damage is extensive, multiple areas are affected, or the flooring has reached the end of its practical lifespan.
WHERE IT'S USED
Where Vinyl Flooring Can Be Used
Vinyl suits homes, offices, retail spaces, and other indoor commercial interiors where a durable, relatively low-maintenance floor is wanted without the cost or weight of natural stone or solid timber. It's a common choice for bedrooms and living areas, as well as office floors that need to handle daily foot traffic without heavy upkeep.
LOCAL CONTEXT
Vinyl Flooring in Singapore
Singapore's humidity and the moisture that comes with it are part of why subfloor condition matters so much before installation, and why floors near bathrooms, kitchens, or windows are worth keeping an eye on over time. Vinyl generally handles moisture better than timber, but that doesn't mean every product or installation is unaffected, which is why the specifics are confirmed against the actual material chosen.

FAQ
Frequently Asked Questions
Can damaged vinyl flooring be repaired?
Often, yes, particularly for localised damage like a tear, a lifted section, or scratches. Whether a repair blends in well depends on the product and how noticeable the affected area is.
When should vinyl flooring be replaced instead of repaired?
When the damage is widespread, when multiple areas are affected, or when the flooring has generally reached the end of its useful life. A repair on a floor that's failing throughout usually isn't a practical fix.
Can vinyl flooring be installed over an existing floor?
In some cases, depending on the condition and type of the existing floor. This is confirmed during an assessment, since installing over an unsuitable surface can affect how the new flooring performs.
How long does vinyl flooring normally last?
This varies with the product quality, how the space is used, and how well it's installed and maintained, rather than a single fixed number. Higher-traffic areas will generally show wear sooner than quieter rooms.
Is vinyl flooring suitable for Singapore homes?
Yes. Vinyl is a common choice in Singapore homes for its durability and relatively low maintenance, and it handles humidity better than timber, though subfloor condition still matters for a good result.
How should vinyl flooring be maintained?
Regular cleaning and prompt attention to any lifting, scratches, or damage go a long way. Leaving a small problem unaddressed is usually what turns it into a larger repair later.
