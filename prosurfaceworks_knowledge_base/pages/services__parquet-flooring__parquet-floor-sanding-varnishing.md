What Is Parquet Floor Sanding & Varnishing?
Preparing the existing parquet surface
Sanding the surface to refine its condition
Removing or refining the existing finish where appropriate
Applying a suitable varnish or finish
Renewing the floor's appearance and surface protection
The specific approach and finish used depend on the condition of the existing floor and what is confirmed during assessment.
When Should You Consider Parquet Sanding & Varnishing?
The existing finish is heavily worn
The surface has become uneven
The old finish has deteriorated
The floor looks significantly aged
The previous coating needs renewal
Polishing can no longer restore the desired finish
Sanding and varnishing addresses the surface and finish. Where the parquet itself has physical damage, see the section on repair below.

Does Your Parquet Need Sanding or Repair?
Sanding & Varnishing may be suitable when:
The main issue is surface or finish deterioration
The parquet structure is generally sound
The existing finish needs renewal
Repair & Restoration may be more appropriate when:
Individual blocks are damaged
Sections are loose
Pieces are missing
Significant localised damage exists

Our Parquet Floor Sanding & Varnishing Process
1. Floor Assessment
The existing parquet and finish condition are assessed.
2. Preparation
The area and surface are prepared, including furniture clearance where applicable.
3. Sanding
The existing surface and finish are sanded as appropriate to the floor's condition.
4. Surface Preparation
The sanded floor is cleaned and prepared for the next stage.
5. Varnishing
A suitable varnish or finish is applied.
6. Drying / Curing
The applied finish is left to dry and cure according to the product used.
7. Final Inspection
The finish consistency and overall result are inspected.

FAQ
Frequently Asked Questions
What is parquet floor sanding and varnishing?
It is a process that prepares an existing parquet surface through sanding and applies a new or renewed varnish finish, addressing a worn or deteriorated finish rather than physical damage.
When does parquet flooring need sanding instead of polishing?
When the existing finish is heavily worn or deteriorated rather than simply dull. For lighter surface wear, see
Parquet Floor Polishing
Can sanding remove old varnish?
Sanding can remove or refine an existing varnish finish as part of the preparation process. Where old varnish itself is the specific concern, see
Old Varnish Removal
How long does parquet sanding and varnishing take?
This depends on the size of the area, the existing floor condition, and the finish selected, and is confirmed after assessment.
How long does the varnish take to dry or cure?
Drying and curing time depends on the specific product used and site conditions, and will be confirmed as part of the process.
Can damaged parquet be sanded and varnished?
This depends on the type of damage. Surface-level wear can often be addressed through sanding and varnishing, but physical damage such as loose or missing pieces may need to be addressed first. See
Parquet Repair & Restoration
for that.
