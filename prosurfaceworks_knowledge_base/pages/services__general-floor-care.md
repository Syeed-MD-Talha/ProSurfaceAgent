RECOGNISE THE SIGNS
When Your Floor Needs More Than Routine Cleaning
A floor that used to mop up fine sometimes stops responding to regular cleaning. The shine doesn't come back the way it used to. Traffic areas near doorways or aisles start to look noticeably different from the rest of the room. Scuff marks and light scratches stay visible no matter how the floor is cleaned.
These are usually signs that the floor's finish, not just its surface dirt, has worn down or aged past what routine mopping can fix. That's the point where polishing, buffing, or refinishing becomes the relevant option rather than another round of cleaning product.
FLOOR TYPES
Hard Floor Surfaces We Care For
Homogeneous tiles: common in HDB and condo units, these show wear as a dulled or scratched glaze, usually addressed with polishing.
Granite: a dense, hard-wearing floor material that still loses its finish over time, particularly in high-traffic commercial areas.
Terrazzo: common in older properties and common areas, terrazzo often needs grinding and polishing to bring back its original aggregate finish.
Concrete: used in warehouses, car parks, and some commercial units, concrete floors are typically refinished rather than polished to a high shine.

CHOOSING BETWEEN THEM
Which Floor Treatment Does Your Surface Need?
Polishing restores shine and appearance where the existing finish is still largely intact.
Buffing maintains or lightly improves the finish, depending on the floor type and how worn it is.
Refinishing addresses floors where the existing finish has broken down and needs more substantial treatment.
The right treatment depends on the flooring material, the existing finish, and the condition of the surface, confirmed on-site rather than guessed from a description over the phone.

WHERE IT'S USED
For Homes, Offices, and Shared Spaces
Homes and HDB or condo units usually see this kind of wear in kitchens, living areas, and corridors, where foot traffic is heaviest. Offices, retail units, and other commercial premises tend to see it faster, simply because more people cross the same floor every day. Common areas and shared lobbies fall somewhere in between, with wear patterns shaped by how the space is actually used rather than its size alone.
LOCAL ENVIRONMENT
Floor Care in Singapore's Everyday Environment
Foot traffic, dust, and grit carried in from outside all play a part in how quickly a hard floor's finish wears down, and Singapore's humidity can add to that over time. This doesn't mean every floor here needs constant attention. It does mean the right maintenance interval depends on how the space is used, not a fixed schedule that applies everywhere.

FAQ
Frequently Asked Questions
How do I know whether my floor needs polishing or refinishing?
If the finish is largely intact and the floor has just lost its shine, polishing is usually enough. If the finish has worn through or the surface has deeper damage, refinishing is the more likely option. A site visit confirms which applies.
Can dull homogeneous tiles be polished?
Yes. Homogeneous tiles are one of the most common floor types this category covers, and a dulled glaze is usually addressed with polishing rather than a full refinish.
Is floor buffing suitable for every type of hard floor?
Not always. Buffing works best when the existing finish is still in reasonable condition. Floors with deeper wear or damage typically need refinishing instead, which is confirmed during the assessment.
Can scratches and scuff marks be removed?
Light scratches and scuff marks can often be polished out. Deeper scratches may need refinishing to fully correct, depending on how far they go into the surface.
How often should hard floors be professionally maintained?
This depends on foot traffic and how the space is used, not a fixed schedule. A commercial floor with heavy daily traffic will typically need attention sooner than a quiet residential room.
Can you assess the condition of an existing floor before recommending a treatment?
Yes. Every job starts with an on-site assessment of the floor's material, finish, and condition, so the recommendation is based on the actual surface rather than a general assumption.
